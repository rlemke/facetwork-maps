How it is scored. The model’s constants were chosen on 2014–2018 and are reported only on later seasons, so these are out-of-sample numbers. No betting line is openly published for the NWSL, so the model is compared with the two predictions a fan makes without one. The Brier score is the mean squared error of the probabilities (lower is better; summed over home win, draw and away win, so always guessing the base rates scores about 0.65). In soccer the model’s most likely result is almost never a draw, so its accuracy is capped by the draw rate (26% of games): the Brier score is the fairer comparison.

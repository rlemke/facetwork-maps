Method. FiveThirtyEight’s published NFL Elo without its quarterback adjustment: home field worth 48 rating points, 25 for coming off a bye, a margin-of-victory multiplier damped for favourites, playoff differences scaled by 1.2, and 40% regression to the mean between seasons. 25 rating points = 1 point on the scoreboard. The constants were chosen on 2005–2014 and are reported from 2015 on, so these numbers are out of sample.

Two different records. The backtest re-scores history game by game using only results before each game. The live record uses predictions stored before kickoff and never rewritten, so it cannot benefit from anything learned afterwards. The betting line is converted to a probability with a 13.45-point standard deviation around the spread.

Playoff odds come from simulating the rest of the season many times, updating ratings inside each simulation, and seeding each simulated season with the league’s tiebreaking procedure. That procedure reproduces all 48 real conference seedings since 2002; its two “combined ranking of points” steps are not implemented, and net points are used where one would have decided a tie.

Known limit. The model cannot see quarterback changes, injuries, weather or coaching. When it and the line disagree by more than a few points, the line usually knows something the model does not.

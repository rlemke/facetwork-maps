How fast are the drivers of AI risk moving?

Nobody can measure the probability that AI destroys humanity. What can be measured is how quickly the things that would drive such a catastrophe are changing: how powerful AI systems are, how long they work without supervision, how good they are at hacking, how much paid work they can do, how much money is racing in, and how many harms are already being reported. This board tracks 8 of those indicators from open data and asks of each one what you would ask of a clock: is it moving faster or slower than it was?

Right now: 4 of 8 indicators are accelerating (General capability (Epoch Capabilities Index), Offensive cyber skill (Cybench), Private investment in AI, Cost of a frontier training run), 2 are slowing (Training compute of frontier models, Expert science knowledge (GPQA Diamond)), and the rest are steady or too new to call. AI researchers surveyed in 2023 put a median 5% chance on an outcome as bad as human extinction. Read the paper for what each indicator means and what it cannot tell you.

How to read this. Every number is recomputed from the sources on each refresh. "Latest best" is the best model measured so far; "pace" is a straight-line fit (on a log scale for things that grow by multiplying) over the last window, compared with the window before. A board full of red does not mean doom is near, and a green one does not mean safety: it says how fast the inputs to the risk are moving. Safety research, regulation and safeguards are not on the board because no open dataset tracks them over time — the paper says what is missing.
Built from open data — Epoch AI, METR (via Epoch AI), Our World in Data / Stanford AI Index, the AI Incident Database with the MIT AI Risk Repository taxonomy, the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists (via Wikipedia) and AI Impacts. Recomputed on every refresh; the text of the paper is filled in from the same numbers. Nothing here is a forecast of doom: see why there is no single number. Source: fwh_airisk.

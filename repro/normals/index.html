Can a published climate normal be recomputed from the data it derives from?

Recomputing NOAA's 1991–2020 U.S. Climate Normals from the GHCN-Daily observations they are built on, across 12 stations, as a Facetwork FFL workflow. September 2026.

1. Purpose of the analysis

A climate normal is the reference against which "hotter than average" is measured — in agriculture, energy demand forecasting, insurance pricing and building codes. NOAA publishes the normals and the daily observations they derive from, so unlike most published results this one can be checked rather than trusted.

The question is not "are the published normals correct". It is narrower and more useful: can an independent party, starting from the same archived observations, arrive at the same numbers? Where the answer is no, the gap measures how much of the published product depends on decisions that are documented in prose but not reproducible from the archive.

The normals carry their own provenance: comp_flag records how each value was derived and years_* how many years contributed. For the stations here that field reads 30 — the full period.

New York's input is a clean control: 10,958 days, 100% complete, zero QC flags. Any difference there is methodological, not missing data.

3. What our FFL workflow found

"Bias" is the mean signed residual (ours − NOAA) where an exact sign test finds the residuals systematically one-sided (p < 0.01 over 12 months). none means the residuals were consistent with noise.

The single-station result was misleading, and the fan-out is what showed it. New York alone gives TMAX high in all 12 months (+0.242) and TMIN low in all 12 (−0.511) — a clean, consistent offset. The obvious explanation is a unit round-trip: US first-order stations record whole degrees F, GHCN-Daily archives tenths of a degree C, and the conversion does not commute.

Twelve stations refute it. TMAX bias ranges -0.899 (Seattle) to +0.242 (New York) — it flips sign. An archive-wide rounding artifact would be uniform. Los Angeles shows no systematic bias in either column; three more show none in one. The difference is station-specific — consistent with per-station handling of missing days, station moves and instrument changes — not an artifact of the archive.

With n = 1 the wrong explanation was not merely possible, it was compelling.

These are not near misses inside a tolerance. They stop at keys: values genuinely outside 2%, Chicago in every month. Whatever produced the published figures there is not recoverable from the archived daily record by averaging it.

The algorithmic point: a tolerance cannot see a bias

Eight of the twelve stations pass at 2% tolerance. Reported as pass/fail, the study would conclude "reproduced" and stop. But at New York every one of 36 values is inside tolerance and every one falls on the same side per column — TMAX high 12/12, TMIN low 12/12.

verdict : agree at level `values`,  differing: 0
TMAX residuals SYSTEMATIC: 12+/0- of 12, mean +0.2425  (p=0.00049)
TMIN residuals SYSTEMATIC: 0+/12- of 12, mean -0.5108  (p=0.00049)

Agrees and differs at once. "36 small random errors" and "36 errors in the same direction" are completely different claims about a reproduction, and only the second says the method differs. A tolerance is a per-value predicate; it cannot express that. So the comparison runs an exact sign test over the signed residuals — Binomial(n, ½) under the null, no distributional assumption — and reports the structure beside the verdict.

The test is per column, never pooled. The first implementation pooled, and TMAX running high cancelled TMIN running low into a balanced sign split reporting "no bias" — while both columns were systematically wrong in opposite directions. The check destroyed the signal it existed to find.

5. Reproducing this

fw ffl run --primary examples/repro-normals/noaa_normals_repro.ffl \
           --library facetwork/ffl/file.ffl \
           --workflow repro.normals.ReproduceManyStations

The recomputation is an FFL script block bound to fw.file.FileOps — no handler and no domain package. The ~18 MB daily file is read inside the script rather than passed through a step payload, which would exceed the 16 MB document limit. foreach … limit 4 bounds how many stations are in flight so the run does not pull twelve large files from NOAA at once.

6. Limitations

  • Our method is deliberately naive. A plain monthly mean is not NOAA's documented procedure, which includes estimation and homogenisation. This measures the gap between the archive and the product; it does not claim the published normals are wrong. Where they differ, the published value is the more considered one.
  • Twelve stations, all US first-order airport/city sites. The pattern may not extend to cooperative-observer stations, which have different reporting practices and more missing data.
  • TAVG is computed as (TMAX+TMIN)/2, which is the conventional definition but not necessarily how the published TAVG normal is derived.
  • The sign test detects that residuals are one-sided. It does not attribute a cause; the station-specific explanations offered above are consistent with the data, not demonstrated by it.

Naan-style flatbread

Soft, blistered skillet flatbreads made with yogurt, brushed with garlic butter.

Dairy-free option 3.4 g net carbs Serves 6 · 1 flatbread prep 15 min · cook 20 min · rest 10 min

Ingredients

  • 1 cup almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (96 g)
  • 2 tbsp coconut flour (14 g)
  • 2 tbsp psyllium husk powder (18 g)
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp fine salt
  • ½ cup plain whole-milk Greek yogurt (114 g)
  • ⅓ cup water (78 g), warm
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (14 g)

Garlic butter

  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter (28 g), melted
  • 1 garlic clove, minced

Method

  1. Whisk the almond flour, coconut flour, psyllium, baking powder and salt.
  2. Stir in the yogurt, warm water and olive oil and knead briefly into a soft dough. Cover and rest 10 minutes.
  3. Divide into 6 and roll each between two sheets of parchment into an oval about 3 mm (⅛ in) thick.
  4. Heat a dry cast-iron pan over medium-high heat. Peel off the top sheet, flip a flatbread into the pan and peel off the second sheet.
  5. Cook 1½–2 minutes per side, until blistered and spotted brown.
  6. Mix the melted butter with the garlic and brush over the hot flatbreads.

Dairy-free version

  • Use unsweetened coconut yogurt instead of plain whole-milk Greek yogurt.
  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

Coconut yogurt is thinner than Greek yogurt; hold back a tablespoon of the water and add it only if the dough feels stiff.

Tips

  • Psyllium gives this dough a bread-like chew; brands differ, and some darken the crumb purple-grey — it is harmless.

Where it comes from

Naan · Delhi · South Asia · medieval

Naan is a leavened flatbread of South and Central Asia, traditionally slapped onto the wall of a clay tandoor oven. It is recorded at the courts of medieval Delhi, and today is closely associated with the cooking of northern India and Pakistan.

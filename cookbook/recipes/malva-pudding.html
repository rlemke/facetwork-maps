Malva pudding

A light, spongy baked pudding that soaks up a hot buttery cream sauce as it comes out of the oven, served warm.

Dairy-free option 3.0 g net carbs Serves 9 · 1 square prep 20 min · cook 45 min · rest 15 min

Ingredients

Apricot jam

  • 2 fresh apricots, stoned and finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp allulose (12 g)
  • 1 tbsp water (15 g)

Pudding

  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • 1½ tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp baking soda
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • ⅓ cup granulated erythritol (63 g)
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter (28 g), melted
  • ½ cup unsweetened almond milk (120 g)
  • 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract

Sauce

  • ½ cup heavy whipping cream (119 g)
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g)
  • ⅓ cup allulose (63 g)
  • ¼ cup water (59 g)

Method

  1. Cook the apricots, allulose and water in a small pan over low heat, stirring and mashing, for 6–8 minutes until thick and jammy. Cool.
  2. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F). Grease a 20 cm (8 in) square baking dish.
  3. Whisk the almond flour, baking powder, baking soda and salt.
  4. Beat the eggs and erythritol until pale and thick, about 3 minutes. Beat in the apricot jam, melted butter, almond milk, lemon juice and vanilla, then fold in the dry ingredients.
  5. Pour into the dish and bake 25–30 minutes, until well risen, golden and a skewer comes out clean.
  6. In the last 5 minutes, heat the cream, butter, allulose and water in a saucepan until the butter melts and it just simmers.
  7. Prick the hot pudding all over with a skewer and pour the hot sauce slowly over it. Leave 15 minutes to soak before serving warm.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.
  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream.

With coconut cream and dairy-free butter the sauce soaks in just as well; the flavour is a little less buttery.

Tips

  • Two fresh apricots cooked down stand in for the spoonful of apricot jam in a traditional batter; allulose browns fast, so keep the heat low.

Where it comes from

Baked sponge pudding · Cape Town, South Africa · Sub-Saharan Africa · 1900s

Malva pudding is an Afrikaans dessert associated with the Cape and its Dutch-descended cooking. Its exact origin and the meaning of the name are not firmly recorded. It became one of South Africa's best-known desserts in the later 1900s and is now served across the country.

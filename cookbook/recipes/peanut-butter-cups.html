Peanut butter cups

Dark chocolate shells around a salty, sweetened peanut butter centre, made in a muffin tin.

Dairy-free 3.5 g net carbs Serves 12 · 1 cup prep 30 min · cook 5 min · rest 1 h

Ingredients

Chocolate

  • 120 g sugar-free dark chocolate chips (stevia or erythritol sweetened)
  • 60 g unsweetened baking chocolate (100% cacao)
  • 1½ tbsp coconut oil (20 g)
  • 3 tbsp powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (24 g)

Filling

  • ½ cup peanut butter, smooth, unsweetened (128 g)
  • 3 tbsp powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (24 g)
  • 2 tsp coconut oil, melted
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • ½ tsp pure vanilla extract

From typical product labels, which vary by brand: powdered erythritol (confectioners' style), sugar-free dark chocolate chips (stevia or erythritol sweetened), unsweetened baking chocolate (100% cacao).

⚠️ Many brands contain milk fat; choose one labelled dairy-free.

Method

  1. Line a 12-hole mini or standard muffin tin with paper cases.
  2. Melt both chocolates with the coconut oil over barely simmering water, then whisk in the powdered erythritol until smooth.
  3. Spoon about 2 tsp chocolate into each case and tilt to coat the base and a little way up the sides. Freeze 10 minutes.
  4. Stir the filling ingredients together until smooth and thick. Roll into 12 balls, flatten each into a disc slightly smaller than the cases, and press one onto each chocolate base.
  5. Cover with the rest of the chocolate, tapping the tin to level it. Refrigerate 1 hour, until firm. Keep chilled.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Check the chocolate chips are dairy-free: many sugar-free brands contain milk fat.

Where it comes from

Peanut butter cup · Hershey, Pennsylvania · North America · 1928

The chocolate peanut butter cup was created in 1928 in Hershey, Pennsylvania, by H. B. Reese, a former employee of the Hershey company who had started his own candy business. It became one of the best-selling sweets in the United States.

Summer pudding

A dome of juice-soaked low-carb sponge packed with lightly cooked summer berries, pressed overnight.

Dairy-free 5.3 g net carbs Serves 8 · 1 wedge prep 40 min · cook 25 min · rest 8 h

Ingredients

Low-carb sponge (in place of bread)

  • 4 large eggs
  • ¼ cup granulated erythritol (48 g)
  • 1 cup almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (96 g)
  • 1 tbsp psyllium husk powder
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 2 tbsp coconut oil (27 g), melted, plus a little for the tin

Fruit

  • 250 g raspberries
  • 125 g redcurrants, stripped from their stalks
  • 125 g blackberries
  • ⅓ cup granulated erythritol (63 g)
  • 3 tbsp water (45 g)

Not low-carb is a rough estimate of a conventional version, not a tested recipe: sugar for granulated erythritol (0.7 × by weight); all-purpose wheat flour for almond flour (blanched, finely ground); no psyllium husk powder. Everything else is as written. Sweeteners are matched for sweetness, and ordinary recipes are often sweeter, so the real difference is usually larger.

Contains: coconut, eggs, tree nuts.

From typical product labels, which vary by brand: granulated erythritol, psyllium husk powder, water.

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F). Oil a 23 × 33 cm (9 × 13 in) baking tin and line it with parchment.
  2. Whisk the eggs and erythritol until pale and thick, about 3 minutes. Whisk the almond flour, psyllium, baking powder and salt together, then fold them in with the melted coconut oil.
  3. Spread thinly and evenly in the tin and bake 15–18 minutes, until springy and lightly golden. Cool completely; this thin sheet stands in for the white bread of the classic pudding.
  4. Put the berries, erythritol and water in a pan and warm over medium-low heat for 3–4 minutes, just until the juices run and the sweetener dissolves. The fruit should keep its shape. Cool slightly.
  5. Line a 1-litre (1-quart) pudding basin with cling film. Cut a round of sponge for the base and strips for the sides, overlapping them slightly. Brush the sponge generously with the berry juice.
  6. Spoon in the fruit with most of the remaining juice, keeping 3–4 tablespoons back. Cover with a lid of sponge.
  7. Fold the film over, set a saucer on top and weigh it down with a couple of cans. Refrigerate at least 8 hours.
  8. Turn out onto a plate and spoon the reserved juice over any pale patches. Cut into wedges.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Almond sponge is softer than stale white bread; pressing it overnight with a weight is what makes it hold its shape, so do not skip it.

Where it comes from

Summer pudding · England · Northern Europe · late 1800s

Summer pudding is an English dessert of stale white bread soaked in the juice of lightly stewed soft fruit and pressed in a basin. It is often said to have begun as a lighter alternative to pastry puddings for health-conscious diners in the late nineteenth century, and it became a classic of the English summer by the early twentieth. The low-carb sponge here replaces the bread.

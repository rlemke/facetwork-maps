Belgian waffles

Crisp-edged, deep-pocketed waffles made light with whipped egg whites folded into an almond-flour batter.

Dairy-free option 3.7 g net carbs Serves 6 · 1 waffle prep 15 min · cook 25 min

Ingredients

  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • 2 tbsp coconut flour (14 g)
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 2 tbsp granulated erythritol (24 g)
  • 4 large eggs, separated
  • ½ cup unsweetened almond milk (120 g)
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g), melted
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • ¼ tsp cream of tartar

Method

  1. Heat a Belgian (deep-grid) waffle iron and grease it well.
  2. Whisk the almond flour, coconut flour, baking powder, salt and erythritol together.
  3. Whisk the egg yolks, almond milk, melted butter and vanilla, then stir into the dry ingredients to make a thick batter. Let it stand 5 minutes so the coconut flour absorbs the liquid.
  4. Whip the egg whites with the cream of tartar to firm peaks. Stir a third into the batter to loosen it, then fold in the rest gently.
  5. Spoon about ½ cup of batter into the iron, close and cook 4–5 minutes, until steam stops and the waffle releases easily. Grain-free waffles stick if opened early.
  6. Cool on a rack for a minute, not on a plate, so the outside stays crisp.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

With dairy-free butter the waffles are a little less browned; cook them 30 seconds longer.

Tips

  • They freeze well; re-crisp straight from frozen in a toaster.

Where it comes from

Waffle · Brussels · Western Europe · medieval

Waffles descend from the thin wafers baked between hinged iron plates in medieval Europe, especially in the Low Countries. The light, deep-grid Brussels waffle became a Belgian speciality in the 1800s, and the name "Belgian waffle" was popularised in the United States after world's fairs in the 1960s.

Bakewell tart

Almond pastry spread with a quick raspberry-chia jam and baked under a moist almond frangipane and toasted flaked almonds.

Dairy-free option 3.3 g net carbs Serves 10 · 1 slice prep 40 min · cook 40 min · rest 1 h

Ingredients

Pastry

  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • 2 tbsp granulated erythritol (24 g)
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g), cold, cubed
  • 1 large egg yolk

Raspberry jam

  • 1 cup raspberries (123 g)
  • 2 tbsp granulated erythritol (24 g)
  • 1 tbsp chia seeds (12 g)

Frangipane

  • ½ cup unsalted butter (114 g), softened
  • ½ cup granulated erythritol (96 g)
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 cup almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (96 g)
  • ½ tsp almond extract
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • 3 tbsp almonds (27 g), flaked

Method

  1. Pulse the pastry ingredients in a food processor until the dough clumps. Press it into a 23 cm (9 in) tart tin with a removable base and chill 20 minutes.
  2. Simmer the raspberries and erythritol, mashing, for 5 minutes. Stir in the chia seeds and cool; the jam thickens as it stands.
  3. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F). Bake the pastry 10 minutes, until just firm, and cool for 10 minutes.
  4. Beat the butter and erythritol until fluffy, then the eggs one at a time. Fold in the almond flour, almond extract and baking powder.
  5. Spread the jam over the pastry, then spoon the frangipane over it and smooth gently to the edges. Scatter the flaked almonds on top.
  6. Bake 25–30 minutes, until golden and springy in the centre; lay foil over it if it browns too fast. Cool at least an hour before slicing.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

Dairy-free butter makes a slightly denser frangipane with a softer crust; it tastes very close to the original.

Tips

  • Spread the jam thinly and keep it away from the very edge, or it bubbles up through the frangipane.

Where it comes from

Bakewell tart · Bakewell, Derbyshire · Northern Europe · 1800s

The town of Bakewell is known first for the Bakewell pudding, a jam-and-egg custard in pastry recorded in the 1800s. A popular story says it began as a cook's mistake at a local inn, but that tale is legend rather than record. The tart, with a firmer almond sponge over the jam, developed later from the pudding and became the familiar British teatime version in the 1900s.

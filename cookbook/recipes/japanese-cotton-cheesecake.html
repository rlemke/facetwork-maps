Japanese cotton cheesecake

A tall, airy soufflé-style cheesecake lightened with whipped egg whites and baked gently in a water bath.

Dairy-free option 3.0 g net carbs Serves 10 · 1 slice prep 35 min · cook 1 h 15 min · rest 3 h

Ingredients

  • 250 g full-fat cream cheese, at room temperature
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter (43 g)
  • ⅓ cup whole milk (81 g)
  • 5 large egg yolks
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice (15 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 2 tbsp coconut flour (14 g)
  • 3 tbsp almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (18 g)
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 5 large egg whites, cold
  • ½ tsp cream of tartar
  • ½ cup powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (60 g)

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 160 °C (320 °F). Line the base and sides of an 18 cm (7 in) round pan with parchment, with the side collar rising 5 cm (2 in) above the rim. If it is a springform, wrap the outside in two layers of foil.
  2. Melt the cream cheese, butter and milk together in a bowl over simmering water, whisking until smooth. Take off the heat and let it cool for 5 minutes.
  3. Whisk in the yolks one at a time, then the lemon juice and vanilla. Sift in the coconut flour, almond flour and salt and whisk until smooth; strain the mixture if it has lumps.
  4. Whisk the egg whites with the cream of tartar until foamy, then add the powdered erythritol a spoonful at a time and whisk to soft, droopy peaks, not stiff ones; over-whipped whites crack the cake.
  5. Fold a third of the whites into the cheese base with a whisk, then fold in the rest gently with a spatula.
  6. Pour into the pan, tap it twice on the counter to release large bubbles, and set it in a roasting tin. Pour in hot water to come 2.5 cm (1 in) up the side.
  7. Bake 20 minutes at 160 °C (320 °F), then lower to 140 °C (285 °F) and bake 50–55 minutes more, until risen, golden and springy.
  8. Turn the oven off and leave the cake inside with the door slightly open for 30 minutes so it does not collapse. Cool, then chill at least 3 hours.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free cream cheese (cashew or almond based) instead of full-fat cream cheese.
  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.
  • Use unsweetened almond milk instead of whole milk.

The dairy-free version rises nearly as well but shrinks more as it cools and is less tangy; it is best eaten the day it is made.

Tips

  • Low-carb flours give less support than wheat, so expect some sinking; a gentle oven and slow cooling help most.

Where it comes from

Cheesecake · Japan · East Asia · 1960s

The light, soufflé-style cheesecake is a Japanese adaptation of European cheesecakes. It is often credited to a Japanese pastry chef who encountered German Käsekuchen on a trip abroad in the 1960s, though the exact story is not well documented. It became a bakery staple in Japan and later spread across East Asia, where it is known as cotton or soufflé cheesecake.

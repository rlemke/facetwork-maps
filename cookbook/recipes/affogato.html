Affogato

A scoop of quick vanilla ice cream 'drowned' in a shot of hot espresso at the table.

Dairy-free option 2.7 g net carbs Serves 4 · 1 scoop with 1 espresso prep 15 min · cook 10 min · rest 6 h

Ingredients

Quick vanilla ice cream

  • 1 cup heavy whipping cream (238 g)
  • 2 large egg yolks
  • ⅓ cup allulose (63 g)
  • 0.0625 tsp fine salt
  • 1½ tsp pure vanilla extract

To serve

  • ½ cup strong brewed coffee or espresso (118 g), 4 freshly made espresso shots

Method

  1. Heat the cream until steaming. Whisk the yolks, allulose and salt, then slowly whisk in the hot cream.
  2. Return to the pan and stir over low heat until it coats a spatula and reaches 80 °C (176 °F). Strain, stir in the vanilla and chill until cold.
  3. Churn in an ice-cream maker, or pour into a shallow container and freeze, stirring hard every 30 minutes for 3 hours. Freeze until firm.
  4. To serve, put a firm scoop into each of four small, chilled cups and pour a hot espresso over it at the table.

Dairy-free version

  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream.

Coconut ice cream melts faster under hot coffee and gives a creamier, coconut-flavoured pool; scoop it straight from a very cold freezer.

Where it comes from

Affogato · Italy · Southern Europe · 1900s

Affogato, Italian for 'drowned', is a scoop of gelato with espresso poured over it. It belongs to Italy's twentieth-century espresso-bar culture; no single inventor or date is documented, and it became widely known outside Italy only from the late twentieth century.

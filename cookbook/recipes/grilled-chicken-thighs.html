Grilled BBQ chicken thighs

Bone-in thighs with a smoky paprika rub, cooked over indirect heat, then brushed with a sugar-free tomato glaze in the last minutes.

Dairy-free 3.9 g net carbs Serves 4 · 2 thighs prep 15 min · cook 40 min · rest 5 min

Ingredients

  • 1300 g bone-in chicken thighs (edible meat and skin), 8 bone-in, skin-on thighs, about 2 kg (4½ lb); about 1.3 kg is meat and skin

Rub

  • 1 tbsp sweet or smoked paprika, smoked
  • 2 tsp fine salt (12 g)
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp onion powder
  • ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp granulated erythritol
  • 1 tbsp avocado oil (14 g)

Glaze

  • 2 tbsp tomato paste (32 g)
  • 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar (30 g)
  • 1 tbsp allulose (12 g)
  • 1 tbsp prepared mustard (yellow or Dijon) (15 g)
  • 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
  • ¼ tsp liquid smoke
  • 3 tbsp water (45 g)

Method

  1. Pat the thighs dry. Mix the rub spices, toss the thighs with the oil, then with the rub. Rest 20 minutes, or refrigerate up to a day.
  2. Whisk the glaze ingredients in a small pan and simmer 2 minutes until slightly thickened.
  3. Set up the grill for two zones: coals or burners on one side only, aiming for about 190 °C (375 °F) under the lid. Lay the thighs skin side up on the cool side.
  4. Cover and cook 30–35 minutes, until a thermometer next to the bone reads 74 °C (165 °F); thighs are juicier taken to 80–85 °C (175–185 °F).
  5. Move the thighs skin side down to the hot side for 2–3 minutes to crisp. Turn, brush with glaze and cook 1–2 minutes more; the glaze contains allulose and scorches fast.
  6. Oven alternative: bake on a rack over a lined tray at 200 °C (400 °F) for 35–40 minutes, brush with glaze and grill under the broiler for 2 minutes.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Worcestershire sauce contains anchovy; tamari is a fish-free swap.
  • Any of the sugar-free barbecue sauces in the sauces chapter can replace this glaze.

On the menu for: Outdoor barbecue.

Where it comes from

Barbecued chicken · United States · North America · 1950s

Grilling chicken over coals and brushing it with sauce became a backyard ritual in post-war suburban America, helped along by cheap charcoal briquettes and the domed kettle grill, which was designed near Chicago in the early 1950s. This is a home cook's dish rather than one with a single birthplace; the map pin marks that grill's home.

Green shakshuka

Eggs poached in a skillet of softened onion, zucchini and spinach — a green take on the North African tomato dish.

Dairy-free 5.9 g net carbs Serves 3 · 2 eggs prep 10 min · cook 25 min

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp olive oil (27 g)
  • ⅓ onion, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ¼ tsp cayenne pepper or chili flakes, or chili flakes, to taste
  • 1 zucchini, diced
  • 200 g fresh spinach
  • ½ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice (15 g)
  • 6 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp fresh mint leaves, chopped
  • 2 tbsp fresh basil leaves, torn
  • 3 tbsp unsweetened coconut yogurt (43 g), to serve

Method

  1. Heat the olive oil in a 25 cm (10 in) skillet over medium heat. Cook the onion 5–6 minutes until soft.
  2. Add the garlic, cumin and cayenne and stir 30 seconds until fragrant, then add the zucchini and cook 5 minutes, stirring, until tender.
  3. Add the spinach a handful at a time, letting each wilt. Season with the salt and pepper and stir in the lemon juice.
  4. Make 6 hollows in the greens and crack an egg into each. Cover and cook over low heat 6–8 minutes, until the whites are set and the yolks still soft.
  5. Scatter with mint and basil and serve with spoonfuls of coconut yogurt.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Squeeze excess liquid from the spinach if using frozen, or the pan will be watery.

Where it comes from

Shakshuka · Tunis · Middle East & North Africa · by the 1900s

Shakshuka — eggs cooked in a spiced sauce, usually of tomatoes and peppers — is a dish of the Maghreb, most often credited to Tunisia. Tunisian and Libyan Jewish communities carried it to Israel, where it became a breakfast staple. Green versions built on leafy vegetables are a modern variation.

Italian ricotta cheesecake

A lighter, slightly grainy crustless cheesecake of ricotta flavoured with lemon and orange zest.

Dairy-free option 6.0 g net carbs Serves 12 · 1 slice prep 20 min · cook 1 h · rest 4 h

Ingredients

  • 900 g whole-milk ricotta, drained in a sieve for 1 hour
  • ⅔ cup granulated erythritol (129 g)
  • 5 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp lemon zest
  • 2 tsp orange zest
  • 2 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp coconut flour
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 1 tsp unsalted butter, for the pan
  • 2 tbsp almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (12 g), for the pan

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 170 °C (340 °F). Butter a 23 cm (9 in) springform pan and dust it with the almond flour.
  2. Beat the drained ricotta and erythritol until as smooth as you can get it, about 2 minutes.
  3. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the zests and vanilla. Sift in the coconut flour and salt and mix just until combined.
  4. Pour into the pan and bake 55–60 minutes, until golden at the edges and set with a slight wobble in the middle.
  5. Turn off the oven and leave the cake inside with the door ajar for 30 minutes, then cool fully and chill at least 4 hours.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free cream cheese (cashew or almond based) instead of whole-milk ricotta — add 2 tbsp almond milk to loosen it.
  • Use olive oil instead of unsalted butter.

With dairy-free cream cheese the cake is smoother and denser, closer to a New York style than to the grainy ricotta original.

Tips

  • Draining the ricotta is essential; wet ricotta gives a soggy, weeping cake.

Where it comes from

Cheesecake · Rome · Southern Europe · ancient

Cakes of fresh cheese were made in ancient Rome; Cato the Elder's farming treatise of the second century BC gives recipes such as libum and placenta that combine cheese with flour and honey. Ricotta-based baked desserts remain common across Italy, especially around Easter, and the ricotta cheesecake is their modern, cake-shaped form.

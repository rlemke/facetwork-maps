Lemon-thyme roast chicken

A whole chicken roasted hot over onions with lemon, garlic and thyme, for a crisp skin and a ready-made pan sauce.

Dairy-free 6.2 g net carbs Serves 4 · a quarter of the bird prep 15 min · cook 1 h 15 min · rest 15 min

Ingredients

  • 1500 g whole chicken (edible meat and skin), one 2.1 kg (4½ lb) chicken; about 1.5 kg is meat and skin
  • 2 tsp fine salt (12 g)
  • ½ tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves, leaves stripped from the stems
  • 2 tsp lemon zest
  • 3 tbsp olive oil (40 g)
  • 1 lemon (flesh), zested first, then halved
  • 6 garlic cloves, lightly crushed in their skins
  • 1 onion, thickly sliced
  • ½ cup chicken stock (120 g)

Contains: none of the common allergen groups.

Method

  1. If you have time, salt the chicken inside and out the night before and leave it uncovered in the fridge. Otherwise salt it an hour before roasting.
  2. Heat the oven to 220 °C (425 °F). Pat the chicken very dry.
  3. Mix the pepper, thyme, lemon zest and 2 tablespoons of the oil and rub it over the bird and under the breast skin. Push the lemon halves and three of the garlic cloves into the cavity.
  4. Spread the onion and the remaining garlic in a 23 × 33 cm (9 × 13 in) pan or a large ovenproof skillet, drizzle with the last tablespoon of oil and set the chicken on top, breast up.
  5. Roast 65–80 minutes, until the skin is deep golden and a thermometer in the thickest part of the thigh reads 74 °C (165 °F) and the juices run clear.
  6. Rest the chicken 15 minutes. Meanwhile set the pan over medium heat, add the stock and scrape up the browned bits; squeeze in the juice from the roasted lemon and simmer 2 minutes for a thin pan sauce.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Dried thyme works too: use 2 teaspoons.
  • Most of the onion's carbs stay in the pan; eat it or not as your count allows.

On the menu for: Family Sunday dinner.

Where it comes from

Roast chicken · France · Western Europe · 1800s

Roast chicken has no single birthplace: birds were spit-roasted across Europe from the Middle Ages onward. This version follows French home cooking, where the Sunday poulet rôti, roasted with butter or oil, garlic and herbs and served with its pan juices, became a standard family meal; the British Sunday roast is its close cousin.

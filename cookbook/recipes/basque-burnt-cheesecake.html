Basque burnt cheesecake

A crustless cheesecake baked very hot so the outside caramelises nearly black while the middle stays soft and creamy.

Dairy-free option 5.3 g net carbs Serves 12 · 1 slice prep 15 min · cook 40 min · rest 4 h

Ingredients

  • 900 g full-fat cream cheese, at room temperature
  • ½ cup allulose (96 g)
  • ½ cup granulated erythritol (96 g)
  • ½ tsp fine salt
  • 5 large eggs, at room temperature
  • 1½ cups heavy whipping cream (357 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp coconut flour

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 220 °C (425 °F). Crumple two large sheets of parchment, flatten them out and use them to line a 23 cm (9 in) springform pan, pleating them so they rise 5 cm (2 in) above the rim.
  2. Beat the cream cheese, allulose, erythritol and salt on low speed until completely smooth.
  3. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the cream and vanilla. Sift the coconut flour over and mix until just smooth.
  4. Pour into the pan and bake 35–40 minutes, until the top is deep brown, almost black in places, and the centre wobbles a lot when you nudge the pan.
  5. Cool to room temperature in the pan; it will sink and crack, which is expected. Refrigerate at least 4 hours, then bring back to room temperature for 30 minutes before serving.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free cream cheese (cashew or almond based) instead of full-fat cream cheese — block-style.
  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream.

The dairy-free version browns and sets well but is softer and less tangy; chill it overnight and serve it cold rather than at room temperature.

Tips

  • Allulose caramelises like sugar and gives the dark top; with erythritol alone the cake stays pale.
  • Do not use a water bath and do not wait for the centre to set; it firms as it cools.

Where it comes from

Cheesecake · San Sebastián · Southern Europe · 1990

The burnt cheesecake comes from La Viña, a bar in the old town of San Sebastián in the Spanish Basque Country, where it was first served in 1990. Baked crustless in a very hot oven, it was deliberately rustic. It spread worldwide in the late 2010s and is now often called 'Basque cheesecake'.

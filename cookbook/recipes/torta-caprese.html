Torta caprese

A flourless chocolate and almond cake from Capri with a crisp top and a soft, almost fudgy middle.

Dairy-free option 3.4 g net carbs Serves 12 · 1 slice prep 25 min · cook 40 min · rest 1 h

Ingredients

  • 140 g unsweetened baking chocolate (100% cacao), chopped
  • ⅔ cup unsalted butter (152 g), cubed
  • 200 g almonds, blanched, toasted and finely ground
  • 2 tbsp unsweetened cocoa powder (11 g)
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 5 large eggs, separated, at room temperature
  • ¾ cup granulated erythritol (144 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp powdered erythritol (confectioners' style), for dusting

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 170 °C (340 °F). Grease a 23 cm (9 in) springform pan and line the base with parchment.
  2. Melt the chocolate and butter together over barely simmering water, stir until smooth and let it cool until just warm.
  3. Mix the ground almonds, cocoa and salt.
  4. Whisk the yolks with half the erythritol until pale and thick, then stir in the chocolate and vanilla, followed by the almond mixture.
  5. Whisk the whites to soft peaks, add the remaining erythritol and whisk to firm peaks. Fold them into the chocolate base in three additions; the first will be hard work, the last should be gentle.
  6. Scrape into the pan and bake 35–40 minutes, until the top is crisp and cracked and a skewer in the centre comes out with moist crumbs.
  7. Cool completely in the pan; the cake sinks slightly as it cools. Unmould and dust with powdered erythritol.

Dairy-free version

  • Use coconut oil instead of unsalted butter (0.85 × the amount) — refined coconut oil has no coconut taste; use a little less because it is all fat.

With coconut oil the cake is a touch firmer and more truffle-like once cold; serve it at room temperature.

Tips

  • Grinding toasted almonds yourself gives the authentic coarse texture; almond flour also works but makes a finer, softer cake.

Where it comes from

Chocolate cake · Capri · Southern Europe · 1900s

Torta caprese is named after the island of Capri in the Bay of Naples. A popular story says a baker forgot to add the flour to an almond and chocolate cake and the result was a success; the tale is a legend and the cake's exact origin is not documented. It is naturally flourless and is widely eaten around Naples and the Amalfi Coast.

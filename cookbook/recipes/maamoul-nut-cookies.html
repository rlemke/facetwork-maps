Ma'amoul-style walnut and pistachio cookies

Buttery almond-flour shells filled with cinnamon walnuts or rosewater pistachios, pressed in a mould or patterned with a fork.

Dairy-free option 2.0 g net carbs Serves 16 · 1 cookie prep 45 min · cook 18 min · rest 30 min

Ingredients

Dough

  • 2 cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (192 g)
  • 2 tbsp coconut flour (14 g)
  • ¼ cup powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (30 g)
  • ½ tsp xanthan gum
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • ⅓ cup unsalted butter (75 g), melted
  • 1 large egg white
  • 2 tsp orange blossom water (10 g)

Walnut filling

  • ½ cup walnuts (58 g), finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp granulated erythritol (12 g)
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tsp orange blossom water

Pistachio filling

  • ⅓ cup pistachios, shelled (41 g), finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp granulated erythritol (12 g)
  • 1 tsp rose water

To finish

  • 2 tbsp powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (16 g)

Method

  1. Mix the almond flour, coconut flour, powdered erythritol, xanthan gum and salt. Stir in the melted butter, egg white and orange blossom water and knead briefly to a smooth, pliable dough. Cover and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Stir each filling together in its own bowl; they should clump when pressed.
  3. Heat the oven to 165 °C (325 °F) and line a baking sheet with parchment.
  4. Divide the dough into 16 balls. Flatten one in your palm to a 7 cm (3 in) round, put a heaped teaspoon of filling in the centre, and close the dough around it, pinching to seal. Use walnut filling for 8 and pistachio for 8.
  5. Press each into a ma'amoul mould and tap out, or flatten slightly and pattern the tops with a fork or tweezers.
  6. Bake 15–18 minutes, until the bottoms are just golden; the tops stay pale. Cool completely, then dust with powdered erythritol.

Dairy-free version

  • Use coconut oil instead of unsalted butter (0.85 × the amount) — melted; traditional ma'amoul often uses ghee or a neutral fat.

Coconut oil makes a slightly crisper, more crumbly shell; keep the dough covered so it doesn't dry out while you fill the cookies.

Tips

  • If the dough cracks as you close it, patch it with a pinch from the next ball; almond dough has no gluten to stretch.
  • Traditional ma'amoul also come with date filling, left out here for the carbs.

Where it comes from

Ma'amoul · The Levant · Middle East & North Africa · by the 1800s

Ma'amoul are filled shortbread cookies shared across Lebanon, Syria, Palestine and Jordan, traditionally made from semolina and pressed in carved wooden moulds whose patterns signal the filling. Families bake them in large batches for Easter and for the feasts at the end of Ramadan. This version keeps the nut fillings and the shape but replaces the semolina shell.

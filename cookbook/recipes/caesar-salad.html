Caesar salad

Crisp romaine in an egg-yolk, lemon and garlic dressing seasoned with anchovy, Worcestershire and parmesan, scattered with almond-flour crisps in place of croutons.

Dairy-free option 4.2 g net carbs Serves 6 · about 2 cups prep 20 min · cook 12 min

Ingredients

Almond crisps

  • ½ cup almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (48 g)
  • 1 large egg white
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (14 g)
  • ¼ tsp garlic powder
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt

Dressing

  • 1 large egg yolk, pasteurised if serving the young, elderly or pregnant
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 4 anchovy fillets in oil, optional; leave out for a milder dressing
  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice (30 g)
  • 2 tsp Worcestershire sauce (11 g)
  • 1 tsp prepared mustard (yellow or Dijon), Dijon
  • ½ cup olive oil (108 g)
  • ½ cup grated parmesan (50 g), divided
  • ½ tsp ground black pepper

Salad

  • 600 g romaine lettuce, chopped, two large hearts, torn or chopped

Not low-carb is a rough estimate of a conventional version, not a tested recipe: all-purpose wheat flour for almond flour (blanched, finely ground). Everything else is as written. Sweeteners are matched for sweetness, and ordinary recipes are often sweeter, so the real difference is usually larger.

Contains: dairy, eggs, fish, tree nuts. Dairy-free version contains: eggs, fish, tree nuts.

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 165 °C (325 °F) and line a baking sheet with parchment. Mix the almond flour, egg white, olive oil, garlic powder and salt into a stiff paste. Spread it thinly, about 3 mm (1/8 in), on the parchment and bake 10–12 minutes, until golden. Cool, then break into crouton-size pieces.
  2. Mash the anchovies and garlic to a paste with the side of a knife, then whisk them in a bowl with the egg yolk, lemon juice, Worcestershire and mustard. Whisk in the olive oil a few drops at a time, then in a thin stream, until thick and glossy. Stir in half the parmesan and the pepper. Thin with a teaspoon of water if needed.
  3. Toss the romaine with the dressing until every leaf is coated. Top with the crisps and the remaining parmesan and serve at once.

Dairy-free version

  • Use nutritional yeast flakes instead of grated parmesan (0.5 × the amount).

Nutritional yeast gives the dressing a savoury, nutty depth but not parmesan's saltiness; add a pinch of salt and another dash of Worcestershire.

Tips

  • Anchovies and Worcestershire both contain fish. Without the anchovies the dressing is milder, and Worcestershire still gives it some savoury depth; Cardini's original is said to have used Worcestershire alone.
  • The crisps keep a week in an airtight jar.

On the menu for: Christmas dinner.

Where it comes from

Caesar salad · Tijuana · Latin America & Caribbean · 1920s

The salad is usually credited to Caesar Cardini, an Italian-born restaurateur who ran a restaurant in Tijuana, Mexico, in the 1920s and is said to have improvised it from what was in his kitchen; the exact year and the details of the story vary, and other people have claimed it. Cardini's own version reportedly relied on Worcestershire sauce rather than whole anchovies.

Cream scones

Tender, lightly sweet scones from almond flour, cold butter and cream, cut in wedges and baked until golden.

Dairy-free option 3.2 g net carbs Serves 8 · 1 scone prep 15 min · cook 20 min · rest 10 min

Ingredients

  • 2 cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (192 g)
  • 2 tbsp coconut flour (14 g)
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • ¼ cup granulated erythritol (48 g)
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g), cold, cubed
  • 1 large egg
  • ⅓ cup heavy whipping cream (79 g), plus 1 tbsp for brushing
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F) and line a baking sheet with parchment.
  2. Mix the almond flour, coconut flour, baking powder, erythritol and salt. Rub in the cold butter with your fingertips until it looks like coarse crumbs.
  3. Whisk the egg, cream and vanilla, add to the bowl and stir with a fork until it comes together into a soft dough.
  4. Pat into a 18 cm (7 in) round about 2.5 cm (1 in) thick and cut into 8 wedges. Chill 10 minutes so they hold their shape.
  5. Separate the wedges on the sheet, brush with a little cream and bake 18–20 minutes, until golden.
  6. Cool on the sheet 5 minutes before moving; they firm up as they cool.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.
  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream.

Coconut cream gives a slightly denser scone with a faint coconut note; dairy-free butter should be very cold, as it softens faster than butter.

Tips

  • Serve with whipped cream and a spoonful of crushed berries.

Where it comes from

Scone · Edinburgh · Northern Europe · 1500s

The scone is Scottish: the word appears in Scottish writing in the early 1500s. Early scones were flat rounds of oats or barley cooked on a griddle and cut into wedges; the oven-baked, risen scone came with baking powder in the 1800s and became part of the British afternoon tea.

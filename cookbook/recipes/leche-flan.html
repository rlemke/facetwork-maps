Leche flan

The Philippine yolk-rich, extra-dense caramel custard, steamed in an oval mould and finished with lemon zest in place of calamansi.

Dairy-free option 1.8 g net carbs Serves 8 · 1 slice prep 20 min · cook 40 min · rest 6 h

Ingredients

Caramel

  • ⅓ cup allulose (63 g)
  • 2 tbsp water (30 g)

Custard

  • 10 large egg yolks
  • 1 cup heavy whipping cream (238 g)
  • ½ cup unsweetened almond milk (120 g)
  • ⅓ cup granulated erythritol (63 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • ½ tsp lemon zest

Method

  1. Cook the allulose and water in a small pan, swirling, until amber. Pour into an oval llanera or an 18–20 cm (7–8 in) round pan and tilt to coat.
  2. Stir the yolks gently with a spoon, without whisking in air. Stir in the cream, almond milk, erythritol, vanilla and lemon zest until the erythritol dissolves.
  3. Strain twice onto the caramel and cover the mould tightly with foil.
  4. Steam over gently simmering water, lid on, for 30–40 minutes, until a knife comes out clean and the centre is just set. Keep the heat low: rapid boiling makes holes.
  5. Cool, then refrigerate at least 6 hours. Run a knife round the edge and invert onto a plate.

Dairy-free version

  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream.

Coconut cream makes a very dense, slightly coconut-flavoured flan, which is close in spirit to the coconut flans of the region.

Tips

  • Save the 10 whites for a meringue or an angel-food-style cake.
  • Straining twice is what gives the smooth, bubble-free texture leche flan is known for.

Where it comes from

Flan · Manila, Philippines · Southeast Asia · Spanish colonial era

Leche flan descends from the Spanish flan brought to the Philippines during more than three centuries of Spanish rule. Filipino cooks made it denser and richer, with many yolks and, later, canned condensed and evaporated milk. A widely repeated story links its yolk-heavy recipe to egg whites used in building mortar, but that remains unproven.

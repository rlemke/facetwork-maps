Bacon-wrapped meatloaf

A beef meatloaf bound with almond flour and egg instead of breadcrumbs, wrapped in bacon and finished with a tangy sugar-free tomato glaze.

Dairy-free 2.9 g net carbs Serves 8 · 1 thick slice prep 20 min · cook 1 h 10 min · rest 10 min

Ingredients

Meatloaf

  • 900 g ground beef (80% lean), 80% lean
  • ½ cup almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (48 g)
  • 2 large eggs
  • 60 g onion, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, grated
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce (17 g)
  • 1 tbsp prepared mustard (yellow or Dijon) (15 g)
  • 2 tbsp fresh flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1½ tsp fine salt
  • ½ tsp ground black pepper
  • 8 slices of bacon, thin slices

Glaze

  • 3 tbsp tomato paste (48 g)
  • 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar (15 g)
  • 1 tbsp allulose (12 g)
  • 1 tsp prepared mustard (yellow or Dijon)
  • 2 tbsp water (30 g)

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180 °C (350 °F). Line a rimmed baking tray with foil and set a rack on it.
  2. In a large bowl beat the eggs, then stir in the almond flour, onion, garlic, Worcestershire, mustard, parsley, thyme, salt and pepper. Add the beef and mix with your hands just until even; overworking makes it tough.
  3. Lay the bacon slices side by side, slightly overlapping, on a sheet of parchment. Shape the meat into a loaf about 23 × 12 cm (9 × 5 in) across the middle of them, then use the parchment to fold the slices up over the top. Roll the loaf seam side down onto the rack.
  4. Bake 45 minutes. Meanwhile whisk the glaze ingredients together.
  5. Brush the glaze over the top and bake 15–25 minutes more, until the bacon is browned and a thermometer in the centre reads 71 °C (160 °F). Tent with foil if the glaze darkens too fast.
  6. Rest 10 minutes before slicing.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Worcestershire sauce contains anchovy; use tamari for a fish-free loaf.
  • Baking on a rack lets the fat drain and the bacon crisp; in a loaf pan it stews.

On the menu for: Family Sunday dinner.

Where it comes from

Meatloaf · United States · North America · late 1800s

Minced meat shaped and baked into a loaf has European forebears, among them German Hackbraten and similar dishes elsewhere, and recipes for chopped-meat loaves appear in American cookbooks by the late 1800s. Meatloaf became an American staple in the first half of the twentieth century, when it was valued as a way to stretch cheap meat; wrapping it in bacon is a later, richer variation.

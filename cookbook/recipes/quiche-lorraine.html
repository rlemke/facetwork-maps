Quiche Lorraine

Smoky bacon set in a nutmeg-scented egg custard in a crisp almond-flour crust — made, as in Lorraine, without cheese.

Dairy-free option 4.3 g net carbs Serves 6 · 1 slice prep 25 min · cook 50 min · rest 15 min

Ingredients

Crust

  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • 1 tbsp coconut flour
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g), cold, cubed
  • 1 large egg

Filling

  • 6 slices of bacon
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 1¼ cups heavy whipping cream (298 g)
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ tsp ground black pepper
  • ⅛ tsp ground nutmeg

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F).
  2. Pulse the almond flour, coconut flour, salt and butter in a food processor until crumbly, add the egg and pulse to a dough. Press evenly into a 23 cm (9 in) tart or pie pan, prick all over and bake 12–15 minutes until lightly golden.
  3. While the crust bakes, cut the bacon into 1 cm (½ in) strips and fry until crisp. Drain on paper towels.
  4. Whisk the eggs, yolk, cream, salt, pepper and nutmeg until smooth but not frothy.
  5. Scatter the bacon over the warm crust and pour in the custard.
  6. Bake 30–35 minutes, until the edge is set and the centre wobbles slightly. Rest 15 minutes before slicing.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.
  • Use full-fat coconut milk (canned) instead of heavy whipping cream.

Coconut milk sets a softer custard with a gentle coconut note that the bacon mostly covers; rest it 30 minutes before slicing.

Tips

  • Cover the crust edge with foil if it browns before the filling sets.

Where it comes from

Quiche · Nancy, Lorraine · Western Europe · by the 1500s

Quiche Lorraine comes from the Lorraine region of north-eastern France, where an open tart of eggs, cream and smoked bacon was originally baked in a bread-dough base; its history is often traced to the 1500s. Cheese, now commonly added, is not part of the traditional recipe. The dish became widely fashionable in Britain and the United States after the Second World War.

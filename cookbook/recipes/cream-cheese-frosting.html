Cream cheese frosting

Tangy, spreadable cream cheese frosting sweetened with powdered erythritol, for carrot cake, spice cakes and cupcakes.

Dairy-free option 1.1 g net carbs Serves 12 · about 2 tbsp prep 10 min

Ingredients

  • 225 g full-fat cream cheese, softened, block style
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g), softened
  • ⅔ cup powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (79 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 1 tbsp heavy whipping cream (15 g), only if needed to loosen

Method

  1. Beat the butter on medium speed until creamy, about 1 minute.
  2. Add the cream cheese and beat just until smooth and combined. Over-beating cream cheese makes the frosting runny.
  3. Add the powdered erythritol, vanilla and salt and beat on low until incorporated, then on medium for 30 seconds.
  4. If it is too stiff to spread, beat in the cream a teaspoon at a time.
  5. Frost a cooled cake. Keep frosted cakes refrigerated.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free cream cheese (cashew or almond based) instead of full-fat cream cheese — firm block style.
  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.
  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream.

Dairy-free cream cheese gives a softer, less tangy frosting; chill it 20–30 minutes before spreading and add a squeeze of lemon juice for tang.

Tips

  • Both cream cheese and butter should be soft but still cool; warm ingredients make a loose frosting.
  • Erythritol can taste slightly cooling here; replacing a quarter of it with allulose softens that.

Where it comes from

Cream cheese frosting · United States · North America · mid-1900s

Cream cheese was first sold commercially in New York State in the 1870s. Frostings made by beating it with butter and sugar became an American staple in the mid-1900s, closely tied to carrot cake and, later, red velvet cake. It remains the classic topping for both.

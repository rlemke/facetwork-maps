Chocolate fudge

Soft, dense chocolate fudge made by reducing cream and allulose, then beating in unsweetened chocolate.

Dairy-free option 1.1 g net carbs Serves 25 · 1 square prep 15 min · cook 20 min · rest 4 h

Ingredients

  • 1 cup heavy whipping cream (238 g)
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g)
  • ⅔ cup allulose (129 g)
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 115 g unsweetened baking chocolate (100% cacao), finely chopped
  • ¼ cup powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (30 g)
  • 1½ tsp pure vanilla extract
  • ½ cup walnuts (58 g), chopped, optional

Everything else is as written. Sweeteners are matched for sweetness, and ordinary recipes are often sweeter, so the real difference is usually larger.

Contains: dairy, tree nuts. Dairy-free version contains: coconut, tree nuts.

From typical product labels, which vary by brand: allulose, powdered erythritol (confectioners' style), unsweetened baking chocolate (100% cacao).

Method

  1. Line a 20 cm (8 in) square pan with parchment, leaving an overhang.
  2. Bring the cream, butter, allulose and salt to the boil in a heavy saucepan, then simmer, stirring often, until reduced by about half and thick enough to coat a spoon, 12–15 minutes. Watch it closely: allulose browns faster than sugar.
  3. Take off the heat and add the chocolate. Leave 1 minute, then stir until smooth.
  4. Beat in the powdered erythritol and vanilla with a wooden spoon for a minute or two, until the mixture thickens and loses some of its shine. Stir in the walnuts.
  5. Spread in the pan, cool, then refrigerate at least 4 hours. Lift out and cut into 25 squares.

Dairy-free version

  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream.
  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

Coconut cream reduces to a slightly softer fudge with a faint coconut flavour; keep it chilled and serve straight from the fridge.

Tips

  • Allulose keeps the fudge soft; this is a creamy refrigerator fudge, not the crumbly crystallised kind.

Where it comes from

Fudge · Poughkeepsie, New York · North America · 1880s

Fudge is an American sweet of the late nineteenth century. One of the earliest accounts links it to students at Vassar College in Poughkeepsie, who made it in the 1880s, and fudge-making soon spread through other women's colleges and seaside resorts. How it got its name is uncertain.

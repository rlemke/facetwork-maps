Almond horchata

A cold, creamy, cinnamon-scented drink made by blending soaked almonds with water and straining, in the spirit of horchata.

Dairy-free 2.7 g net carbs Serves 4 · 1 glass prep 15 min · rest 8 h

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup almonds (107 g), blanched
  • 4 cups water (948 g), divided
  • ¾ tsp ground cinnamon
  • 3 tbsp granulated erythritol (36 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 0.0625 tsp fine salt

Method

  1. Put the almonds in a bowl with 2 cups of the water and the cinnamon. Cover and soak in the fridge overnight, or at least 8 hours.
  2. Tip the almonds and soaking water into a blender, add the remaining water, the erythritol, vanilla and salt, and blend on high for 2 minutes, until very smooth.
  3. Strain through a nut-milk bag or a sieve lined with a double layer of muslin, squeezing out as much liquid as you can.
  4. Taste for sweetness, then chill. Stir and serve over ice with a dusting of cinnamon.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Most of the almond solids stay in the bag, so the drink carries far fewer carbs than the whole nuts would; the figure shown counts the whole almonds and so overstates it.
  • The leftover almond pulp can be dried in a low oven and used like almond meal.

Where it comes from

Horchata · Valencia, Spain · Southern Europe · medieval

This is an almond version, not a traditional horchata. The classic Spanish horchata de chufa comes from Valencia and is made from tiger nuts, the small tubers of a sedge grown there since Moorish times; Mexican horchata is made from rice, usually with cinnamon. The name is generally traced to the Latin for barley, an older base for such drinks. Almonds give a similar milky, nutty drink without the starch.

Blondies

Chewy brown-butter bars with toasted pecans and sugar-free chocolate chips, the vanilla cousin of the brownie.

Dairy-free option 1.5 g net carbs Serves 16 · 1 square prep 15 min · cook 25 min · rest 30 min

Ingredients

  • ½ cup unsalted butter (114 g)
  • ½ cup granulated erythritol (96 g)
  • ¼ cup allulose (48 g), gives the butterscotch flavour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • 1 tbsp coconut flour
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • ⅓ cup pecans (36 g), toasted and chopped
  • ⅓ cup sugar-free dark chocolate chips (stevia or erythritol sweetened) (56 g)

⚠️ Many brands contain milk fat; choose one labelled dairy-free.

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 165 °C (325 °F). Line a 20 cm (8 in) square pan with parchment.
  2. Melt the butter in a light-coloured pan and keep cooking, swirling, until it smells nutty and the solids turn brown. Pour into a bowl and cool 10 minutes.
  3. Whisk in the erythritol and allulose, then the eggs and vanilla.
  4. Stir in the almond flour, coconut flour, baking powder and salt, then the pecans and chocolate chips.
  5. Spread in the pan and bake 22–26 minutes, until golden and just set in the centre. Allulose browns fast; tent with foil if the top darkens early.
  6. Cool completely, then chill 30 minutes before cutting.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter — melt it but don't try to brown it.

Dairy-free butter can't be browned, so the bars lose the toffee note; add an extra ½ teaspoon of vanilla and toast the pecans well.

Where it comes from

Brownie · United States · North America · 1900s

Butterscotch bars made with brown sugar and no chocolate appeared in American cookbooks in the first half of the twentieth century, sometimes as 'butterscotch brownies'. The name blondie came into wide use later in the century as the bar became a bake-sale staple alongside the brownie.

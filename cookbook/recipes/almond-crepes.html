Crêpes

Thin, flexible egg-based crêpes held together with a little psyllium, for folding around sweet or savoury fillings.

Dairy-free option 1.8 g net carbs Serves 4 · 2 crêpes prep 10 min · cook 20 min · rest 10 min

Ingredients

  • 4 large eggs
  • ½ cup unsweetened almond milk (120 g)
  • ¼ cup almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (24 g)
  • 1 tbsp coconut flour
  • 1 tsp psyllium husk powder
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter (28 g), melted, plus extra for the pan

Method

  1. Blend all the ingredients until completely smooth, about 30 seconds.
  2. Let the batter rest 10 minutes so the psyllium and coconut flour hydrate; it should be like thin cream. Add a spoonful of water if it is thicker.
  3. Heat a 20 cm (8 in) non-stick pan over medium heat and brush with butter.
  4. Pour in 3 tablespoons of batter and swirl at once to coat the base thinly.
  5. Cook about 1 minute, until the edge lifts and the underside is lightly golden. Loosen with a thin spatula, flip and cook 20–30 seconds more.
  6. Stack on a plate and repeat, stirring the batter each time.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

Dairy-free butter gives slightly paler crêpes but the texture is the same.

Tips

  • Fill with whipped cream and a few berries, or with ham and spinach for a savoury version.

Where it comes from

Crêpe · Quimper, Brittany · Western Europe · medieval

Brittany is the home of the crêpe. Buckwheat, which grows on poor soil, spread there in the late Middle Ages, and thin buckwheat galettes became a staple; sweeter wheat crêpes followed as white flour became affordable. Crêperies carried the dish across France and beyond.

Vegan lemon tart

A crisp almond shortbread shell filled with a sharp, smooth coconut lemon cream set with agar; vegan as written.

Dairy-free Vegan 4.4 g net carbs Serves 10 · 1 slice prep 40 min · cook 25 min · rest 4 h

Ingredients

Shortbread crust

  • 1¾ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (168 g)
  • 3 tbsp powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (24 g)
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 4 tbsp dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) (56 g), cold, cubed; or 3 tbsp solid coconut oil
  • 1 flax egg (1 tbsp golden flaxseed meal + 3 tbsp water, rested 10 min), made 10 minutes ahead

Lemon cream

  • 1½ cups full-fat coconut milk (canned) (339 g), full-fat, well shaken
  • ½ cup coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) (120 g)
  • ½ cup powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (60 g)
  • 2 tbsp allulose (24 g), keeps the cream from turning gritty in the fridge
  • 1¾ tsp agar powder
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • ⅛ tsp ground turmeric, a pinch, for colour only
  • 1 tbsp lemon zest, from about 2 lemons
  • ½ cup fresh lemon juice (122 g), fresh, at room temperature

Method

  1. Make the flax egg and let it rest 10 minutes until gel-like.
  2. Pulse the almond flour, powdered erythritol and salt in a food processor. Add the cold vegan butter and the flax egg and pulse until the dough clumps when pinched. Press it evenly into a 23 cm (9 in) tart tin with a removable base, prick the base and chill 20 minutes.
  3. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F). Bake the shell 14–18 minutes until evenly golden and firm at the edges, covering the rim with foil if it darkens first. Cool completely in the tin.
  4. In a saucepan, whisk the agar into the cold coconut milk until no specks remain, then whisk in the coconut cream, powdered erythritol, allulose, salt, turmeric and zest.
  5. Bring to a full boil over medium heat, whisking, and boil 2 minutes so the agar dissolves completely; the mixture will look thin.
  6. Take the pan off the heat, wait 1 minute, then whisk in the lemon juice. Adding it after the boil keeps the acid from weakening the agar.
  7. Strain at once through a fine sieve into the cooled shell and smooth the top. Work promptly: agar begins to set around 40 °C (104 °F).
  8. Cool 30 minutes at room temperature, then refrigerate at least 3 hours, until the cream is set and cuts cleanly. Slice with a knife dipped in hot water.

Tips

  • Agar sets firmer and cleaner than an egg curd, so the slice is more like a soft panna cotta than a wobbly custard; for a softer cream use 1.5 tsp agar.
  • If the cream does not set, the agar did not boil long enough: return it to the pan, boil 2 minutes more, add a squeeze of lemon off the heat and pour again.
  • Taste the mixture before pouring: coconut mutes lemon more than butter and eggs do, so add more zest rather than more juice if you want it sharper.

Where it comes from

Lemon tart · France · Western Europe · by the 1900s

Lemon creams baked in pastry were made in French and English kitchens long before anyone recorded who first made them, and the tarte au citron became a fixture of French pâtisseries without a single credited inventor. The classic cream is set by eggs and enriched with butter. Egg-free versions that set coconut or plant milk with agar, a seaweed gel long used in East Asian sweets, spread with vegan baking in the 2000s and 2010s.

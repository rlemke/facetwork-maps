Chocolate cookie crumb crust

A dark cocoa and almond-flour press-in crust that stands in for the crushed chocolate-wafer crusts of American icebox pies.

Dairy-free option 2.2 g net carbs Serves 8 · 1/8 of the crust prep 10 min · cook 12 min · rest 30 min

Ingredients

  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • ¼ cup unsweetened cocoa powder (22 g)
  • ¼ cup granulated erythritol (48 g)
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 5 tbsp unsalted butter (71 g), melted
  • ½ tsp pure vanilla extract

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F). Have ready a 23 cm (9 in) pie dish or springform pan.
  2. Whisk the almond flour, cocoa, erythritol and salt together, pressing out any lumps of cocoa.
  3. Pour in the melted butter and vanilla and mix until evenly moistened and dark all the way through.
  4. Press firmly over the base and up the sides of the pan (or over the base only, for a cheesecake). A flat-bottomed glass gives a compact, even layer.
  5. Bake 10–12 minutes. It will still feel soft; it crisps as it cools. Cocoa hides browning, so go by time and smell, and take it out if it smells scorched.
  6. Cool to room temperature, then chill at least 30 minutes before adding a no-bake filling.

Dairy-free version

  • Use coconut oil instead of unsalted butter — melted; or use vegan_butter.

With coconut oil the crust is crisper straight from the fridge but softens quickly at room temperature, so serve chilled fillings cold.

Tips

  • For a crumb you can sprinkle on top, bake a little of the mixture loose on a tray and break it up once cool.

Where it comes from

Crumb crust · United States · North America · early 1900s

Pie shells made from crushed crackers or cookies bound with butter became common in American home baking in the first half of the 1900s, prized because they need little or no baking. Chocolate wafer crumbs became the usual base for cream and icebox pies. This version has no cookies to crush, so it bakes a cocoa and almond-flour mixture that ends up with a similar crumbly, dark crust.

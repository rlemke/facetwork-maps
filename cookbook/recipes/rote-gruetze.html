Rote Grütze (red berry pudding)

Tart redcurrants and raspberries cooked just until they burst, softly set and served cold with a pour of cream.

Dairy-free option 6.0 g net carbs Serves 6 · 1/2 cup prep 15 min · cook 10 min · rest 4 h

Ingredients

Berries

  • ½ cup water (118 g)
  • ⅓ cup allulose (63 g)
  • 180 g redcurrants, stripped from their stems
  • 150 g raspberries
  • 50 g sweet cherries, pitted, pitted and halved
  • 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1½ tsp unflavored powdered gelatin
  • 3 tbsp water (45 g), cold, for the gelatin

To serve

  • ½ cup heavy whipping cream (119 g), lightly whipped or poured

Method

  1. Sprinkle the gelatin over the 3 tablespoons of cold water and leave it to swell for 5 minutes.
  2. Bring the half cup of water and the allulose to a simmer in a saucepan. Add the redcurrants and cherries and cook 3–4 minutes until the currants start to burst.
  3. Add the raspberries and lemon juice and simmer 1 minute more, just until they soften; keep some of the fruit whole.
  4. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the swollen gelatin until it has completely dissolved.
  5. Divide among six small glasses or bowls and chill at least 4 hours, until softly set.
  6. Serve cold with a spoonful of cream on top.

Dairy-free version

  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream.

The pudding itself is dairy-free; chilled coconut cream, stirred smooth, replaces the cream and adds a light coconut note.

Tips

  • The traditional thickener is potato or corn starch; gelatin gives a softer, wobbly set instead of a starchy one. For a looser, sauce-like texture use 1/4 tsp xanthan gum whisked in off the heat.
  • Frozen redcurrants and raspberries work well; cook them a minute longer.

Where it comes from

Fruit pudding · Hamburg, northern Germany · Northern Europe · by the 1800s

Grütze means groats, and the dish began as a grain porridge coloured and flavoured with berry juice before starch-thickened fruit versions took over. Rote Grütze is a regional classic of northern Germany, especially Hamburg and Schleswig-Holstein, and Denmark has a close twin, rødgrød med fløde, served the same way with cream.

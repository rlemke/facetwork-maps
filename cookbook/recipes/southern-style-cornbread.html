Southern-style "cornbread" (no corn)

An unsweetened skillet bread with a crisp, buttery crust, in the Southern style — made without any corn.

Dairy-free option 2.7 g net carbs Serves 9 · 1 wedge prep 10 min · cook 25 min · rest 10 min

Ingredients

  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g), divided
  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • 3 tbsp coconut flour (21 g)
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • ¼ tsp baking soda
  • ¾ tsp fine salt
  • 3 large eggs
  • ½ cup unsweetened almond milk (120 g)
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice (15 g)

Method

  1. Put 1 tablespoon of the butter in a 25 cm (10 in) cast-iron skillet and heat it in the oven as it heats to 200 °C (400 °F).
  2. Melt the remaining butter. Stir the lemon juice into the almond milk and leave 5 minutes.
  3. Whisk the almond flour, coconut flour, baking powder, baking soda and salt.
  4. Beat the eggs, soured almond milk and melted butter, then stir into the dry ingredients. Rest 2 minutes so the coconut flour thickens the batter.
  5. Take out the hot skillet, swirl the butter to coat and pour in the batter; it should sizzle.
  6. Bake 20–25 minutes, until golden and a skewer comes out clean. Cool 10 minutes, then cut into 9 wedges.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

With dairy-free butter the crust is crisp but less nutty; a spoonful of bacon fat in the skillet, if you eat it, adds the traditional flavour.

Tips

  • Honestly, it tastes like a savoury muffin rather than corn; serve it where cornbread would go — with chili, greens or soup.

Where it comes from

Cornbread · Charleston, South Carolina · North America · 1700s

Cornbread comes from Native American maize cooking, which European settlers adopted in North America. In the American South it became an everyday bread: usually unsweetened, made with buttermilk and baked in a hot, greased cast-iron skillet for a crisp crust. This version keeps the method but contains no corn.

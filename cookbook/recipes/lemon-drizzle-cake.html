Lemon drizzle cake

A tender lemon loaf soaked while warm with a sharp lemon syrup that dries to a thin, crackly crust.

Dairy-free option 3.1 g net carbs Serves 10 · 1 slice prep 20 min · cook 45 min · rest 30 min

Ingredients

Loaf

  • ½ cup unsalted butter (114 g), softened
  • ½ cup granulated erythritol (96 g)
  • 4 large eggs, at room temperature
  • 2 tbsp lemon zest (12 g), from about 2 lemons
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 2 cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (192 g)
  • 2 tbsp coconut flour (14 g)
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ cup sour cream (58 g)

Drizzle

  • 3 tbsp fresh lemon juice (45 g)
  • ⅓ cup powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (40 g)

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 170 °C (340 °F). Grease a 23 × 13 cm (9 × 5 in) loaf pan and line it with a parchment sling.
  2. Beat the butter, erythritol and lemon zest until light and fluffy. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla.
  3. Whisk the almond flour, coconut flour, baking powder and salt together, fold half into the batter, then the sour cream, then the rest.
  4. Scrape into the pan and bake 40–45 minutes, until a skewer comes out clean. Cover loosely with foil after 25 minutes if the top is browning fast.
  5. While the loaf bakes, stir the lemon juice and powdered erythritol together; the sweetener will not fully dissolve, which is what gives the crunchy top.
  6. As soon as the loaf comes out, prick it all over with a skewer and spoon the drizzle slowly over it. Leave in the pan until completely cool, then lift out.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.
  • Use unsweetened coconut yogurt instead of sour cream.

With dairy-free butter and coconut yogurt the loaf is slightly moister and less rich; it slices best the next day.

Tips

  • Let the loaf cool fully in the pan: warm almond cake breaks if you lift it out early.

Where it comes from

Loaf cake · Britain · Northern Europe · late 1900s

Lemon-flavoured pound cakes and seed cakes have a long history in British baking, but the drizzle cake, soaked with sharp lemon syrup straight from the oven, became a household standard in the later twentieth century. It is now one of the most popular cakes in Britain, a staple of cafés, bake sales and home kitchens.

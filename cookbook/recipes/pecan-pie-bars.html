Pecan pie bars

A gooey, caramel-like pecan filling on a short almond crust, cut into squares instead of slices.

Dairy-free option 1.3 g net carbs Serves 16 · 1 square prep 20 min · cook 40 min · rest 2 h

Ingredients

Crust

  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • 2 tbsp granulated erythritol (24 g)
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ cup unsalted butter (57 g), melted

Filling

  • ¼ cup unsalted butter (57 g), melted
  • ½ cup allulose (96 g)
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 1½ cups pecans (164 g), roughly chopped

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F). Line a 20 cm (8 in) square pan with parchment.
  2. Mix the crust ingredients, press into the pan and bake 12 minutes, until lightly golden. Lower the oven to 160 °C (325 °F), since allulose browns faster than sugar.
  3. Whisk the melted butter, allulose, eggs, vanilla and salt until smooth. Stir in the pecans.
  4. Pour over the crust and spread evenly. Bake 25–30 minutes, until the edges are set and the centre jiggles only slightly. Tent with foil if the nuts darken too quickly.
  5. Cool completely, then chill 2 hours before cutting into 16 squares.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter — use a firm stick-style one.

With dairy-free butter the filling sets a little softer and tastes less like toffee; a pinch more salt helps.

Tips

  • Allulose is essential here: it gives the syrupy texture of a corn-syrup filling.

Where it comes from

Pecan pie · Southern United States · North America · early 1900s

Pecans are native to the southern United States, and pecan pie is a Southern classic whose exact origin is unknown. It became widespread in the early twentieth century, especially after corn-syrup makers promoted a recipe in the 1930s. Bars baked in a sheet pan are a more recent way of serving it to a crowd.

Chewy ginger molasses cookies

Soft, crackle-topped spice cookies with a chewy middle, warm with ginger, cinnamon and cloves and a true molasses flavour from a single spoonful.

Dairy-free option 2.1 g net carbs Serves 20 · 1 cookie prep 20 min · cook 12 min · rest 45 min

Ingredients

  • 2¼ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (216 g)
  • 2 tbsp coconut flour (14 g)
  • ¾ tsp baking soda
  • ¼ tsp xanthan gum
  • 2 tsp ground ginger
  • 1½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ¼ tsp ground cloves
  • ¼ tsp ground allspice
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • ½ cup unsalted butter (114 g), softened
  • ⅓ cup allulose (63 g)
  • ¼ cup granulated erythritol (48 g)
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tbsp blackstrap or dark molasses (20 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 2 tbsp granulated erythritol (24 g), for rolling

Method

  1. Whisk together the almond flour, coconut flour, baking soda, xanthan gum, spices and salt.
  2. Beat the butter with the allulose and the 1/4 cup erythritol until creamy, then beat in the egg, molasses and vanilla.
  3. Stir in the dry ingredients to a soft dough. Cover and chill 30 minutes so it can be rolled.
  4. Heat the oven to 165 °C (325 °F) and line two baking sheets with parchment.
  5. Roll the dough into 20 balls, roll each in the remaining erythritol and set 5 cm (2 in) apart. Press each down slightly.
  6. Bake 10–12 minutes, until the tops crack and the edges are set but the centres still look soft. Allulose browns fast, so watch the last few minutes.
  7. Cool 10 minutes on the sheet before moving; they firm up as they cool but stay chewy.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

A stick-style dairy-free butter works one-for-one; the cookies spread a little more, so chill the dough the full time.

Tips

  • Molasses is real sugar (about 15 g of carbohydrate per tablespoon), so the whole batch gets just one tablespoon — under 1 g per cookie. It is there for the dark, slightly bitter flavour that no sweetener copies; don't add more.
  • Allulose is what keeps the centres chewy; an all-erythritol version bakes up crisp, more like a gingersnap.

Where it comes from

Molasses cookie · New England · North America · 1800s

Molasses, shipped in from the Caribbean sugar trade, was the everyday sweetener of colonial and nineteenth-century New England, far cheaper than refined sugar. Ginger-spiced molasses cookies and gingerbreads became traditional household baking there and spread across the United States.

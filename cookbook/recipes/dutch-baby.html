Dutch baby

A big oven pancake that puffs in a hot skillet and settles into a custardy centre, served with lemon and powdered sweetener.

Dairy-free option 3.4 g net carbs Serves 4 · 1 wedge prep 10 min · cook 20 min

Ingredients

  • 4 large eggs
  • ½ cup whole milk (122 g)
  • ⅓ cup almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (32 g)
  • 1 tbsp coconut flour
  • 1 tbsp granulated erythritol (12 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter (43 g)
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice (15 g), to serve
  • 1 tbsp powdered erythritol (confectioners' style), to serve

Method

  1. Put a 25 cm (10 in) cast-iron or ovenproof skillet in the oven and heat to 220 °C (425 °F).
  2. Blend the eggs, milk, almond flour, coconut flour, erythritol, vanilla and salt until smooth and frothy, about 1 minute.
  3. Take the hot skillet out, add the butter and swirl until melted and foaming. Pour in the batter at once.
  4. Bake 15–18 minutes without opening the door, until puffed and well browned at the edge.
  5. Squeeze lemon juice over, dust with powdered erythritol and cut into wedges straight away; it sinks as it cools.

Dairy-free version

  • Use unsweetened almond milk instead of whole milk.
  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

Without gluten it rises less than a wheat Dutch baby — expect puffed edges and a flatter, custardy middle. The dairy-free version browns a little less.

Tips

  • A very hot pan matters more than anything; do not skip preheating it.

Where it comes from

Dutch baby · Seattle · North America · early 1900s

The Dutch baby is an American dish adapted from German oven pancakes; "Dutch" is generally taken as a corruption of Deutsch. It is often credited to a Seattle family café in the early 1900s, where the name is said to have been coined.

Butter tarts

Small almond-pastry shells filled with a runny, caramel-like butter filling and a few toasted pecans.

Dairy-free option 1.6 g net carbs Serves 12 · 1 tart prep 35 min · cook 20 min · rest 30 min

Ingredients

Pastry

  • 2 cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (192 g)
  • 1 tbsp granulated erythritol (12 g)
  • ½ tsp xanthan gum
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
  • 5 tbsp unsalted butter (71 g), cold, cubed
  • 1 large egg

Filling

  • ½ cup allulose (96 g)
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter (43 g), melted
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • ⅓ cup pecans (36 g), toasted, chopped

Method

  1. Pulse the almond flour, erythritol, xanthan gum and salt in a food processor. Add the butter and egg and pulse until the dough forms a ball. Chill 15 minutes.
  2. Heat the oven to 165 °C (325 °F). Divide the dough into 12 pieces and press each into a cup of a standard 12-cup muffin tin, up the sides to about 2 cm (3/4 in) high.
  3. Bake the shells 8 minutes, until just set.
  4. Whisk the allulose, melted butter, egg, vanilla and salt until smooth. Sprinkle the pecans into the shells and pour the filling over them, about two-thirds full.
  5. Bake 12–15 minutes, until the filling bubbles and the pastry is golden; the centres should still be slightly runny. Cool 30 minutes in the tin before lifting out with a thin knife.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.

Dairy-free butter gives a slightly less toffee-like filling, and the shells are a little more crumbly; let them cool fully in the tin.

Tips

  • Allulose darkens quickly; the lower 165 °C oven keeps the filling from scorching before the pastry is done.
  • Whether butter tarts should hold raisins is a long-running Canadian argument; pecans are the low-carb side of it.

Where it comes from

Butter tart · Barrie, Ontario · North America · 1900

Butter tarts are one of the few desserts widely thought of as Canadian. The earliest known printed recipe is usually traced to a 1900 community cookbook from Barrie, Ontario, though home cooks were likely making them before then. They are related to other syrup-and-butter pastries such as the Scottish ecclefechan tart and the American pecan pie.

Lemon syllabub

Cream whipped with white wine, lemon and a little nutmeg until it barely holds a trail: an old English dessert that takes ten minutes.

Dairy-free option 2.5 g net carbs Serves 6 · 1 small glass prep 15 min · rest 2 h

Ingredients

  • 2 tsp lemon zest
  • 3 tbsp fresh lemon juice (45 g)
  • ¼ cup dry white wine (59 g)
  • ¼ cup powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (30 g)
  • 1½ cups heavy whipping cream (357 g), cold
  • ⅛ tsp ground nutmeg, freshly grated if you have it, plus a little to finish

Method

  1. Stir the lemon zest, lemon juice, white wine and powdered erythritol together in a large bowl and leave for 15 minutes so the zest flavours the liquid.
  2. Pour in the cold cream and add the nutmeg.
  3. Whisk slowly, by hand or on low speed, until the cream thickens enough to hold soft folds. Stop before it is stiff: the acid makes it thicken suddenly, and it goes grainy if pushed further.
  4. Spoon into small glasses, grate a little more nutmeg over, and chill for 1–2 hours before serving.

Dairy-free version

  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream — chill the can overnight and use only the solid part.

Coconut cream gives a thicker, denser syllabub with a coconut note and does not separate on standing the way dairy syllabub can.

Tips

  • Older recipes used sweet wine or sherry; a dry white keeps the carbs down, and dry sherry works the same way.

Where it comes from

Syllabub · England · Northern Europe · 1500s

Syllabub was popular in England from the sixteenth century onward. Early versions were frothy drinks of cream or milk mixed with wine or cider; over the following two centuries cooks began whipping it into a thicker cream that could be eaten with a spoon, and by the 1700s this 'everlasting' syllabub was a fashionable dessert.

Memphis dry-rub ribs

Baby back ribs coated in a paprika-heavy spice rub, cooked slowly until tender and finished with a dusting of more rub instead of sauce.

Dairy-free 3.5 g net carbs Serves 4 · half a rack prep 20 min · cook 3 h 30 min · rest 10 min

Ingredients

  • 1100 g baby back ribs (edible meat), two racks, about 2 kg (4½ lb) total; about 1.1 kg is meat

Rub

  • 2 tbsp sweet or smoked paprika (14 g)
  • 1 tbsp granulated erythritol (12 g)
  • 1 tbsp fine salt (18 g)
  • 2 tsp ground black pepper
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 2 tsp onion powder
  • 1 tsp chili powder
  • 1 tsp dry mustard powder
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • ½ tsp dried thyme
  • ½ tsp cayenne pepper or chili flakes

Mop

  • ½ cup apple cider vinegar (120 g)
  • ¼ cup water (59 g)

Method

  1. Pull the thin membrane off the bone side of each rack: lift a corner with a knife and peel it away with a paper towel. Pat the ribs dry.
  2. Mix the rub. Set aside about 2 tablespoons for finishing and season both sides of the racks with the rest. Rest 30 minutes, or refrigerate overnight.
  3. Smoker: heat to 120 °C (250 °F). Smoke the ribs bone side down, mopping with the vinegar and water every hour, until the meat has pulled back from the bones and a rack bends easily when lifted from one end, 4–5 hours.
  4. Oven: heat to 150 °C (300 °F). Wrap each rack tightly in foil with 2 tablespoons of the mop and bake on a tray for 2 hours. Unwrap, mop and bake uncovered 30–45 minutes more, until the surface is dry and dark.
  5. Ribs are done at 88–93 °C (190–200 °F) between the bones, well past the 63 °C (145 °F) safe point. Mop once more and dust with the reserved rub.
  6. Rest 10 minutes and cut between the bones.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • In Memphis, 'dry' ribs come with sauce on the side if you ask; serve a sugar-free sauce separately rather than brushing it on.
  • The Memphis dry rub in the sauces chapter can replace this rub.

On the menu for: Outdoor barbecue.

Where it comes from

Barbecue · Memphis · North America · mid-1900s

Memphis barbecue centres on pork, especially ribs and chopped shoulder, and the city's pitmasters have long served them either 'wet' with a tomato sauce or 'dry' with only a spice rub. The dry-rub rib is often credited to the Rendezvous, a downtown restaurant that opened in 1948, though rubs were in use before it.

Mashed cauliflower

Steamed cauliflower whipped with butter, cream and garlic until smooth enough to stand in for mashed potatoes.

Dairy-free option 5.3 g net carbs Serves 6 · about 2/3 cup prep 10 min · cook 15 min

Ingredients

  • 900 g cauliflower, one large head, cut into florets
  • 3 garlic cloves, peeled
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter (43 g)
  • ¼ cup heavy whipping cream (60 g), warmed
  • ¾ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ tsp ground black pepper
  • 2 tbsp fresh chives, chopped

Nothing here is a low-carb stand-in, so a conventional version would be much the same.

Contains: dairy. Dairy-free version contains: coconut.

Method

  1. Steam the cauliflower and garlic over boiling water for 12–15 minutes, until a knife slides through the thickest stem with no resistance.
  2. Tip into a colander and let it steam-dry for 5 minutes; wet cauliflower makes a runny mash. Press gently with a clean towel to remove more water.
  3. Blend or process the hot cauliflower and garlic with the butter, warm cream, salt and pepper until smooth, scraping down once. For a rougher texture use a potato masher instead.
  4. Taste for salt, spoon into a warm dish and scatter the chives over the top.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter.
  • Use full-fat coconut milk (canned) instead of heavy whipping cream — use the thick part from the top of the can.

Coconut milk gives a faintly sweet note that the garlic mostly hides; a little extra salt and pepper balances it.

Tips

  • Make it ahead and reheat in a covered dish at 175 °C (350 °F) for 20 minutes, stirring once.

On the menu for: Thanksgiving dinner.

Where it comes from

Mashed potatoes (low-carb imitation) · London · Western Europe · 1700s

Recipes for mashing boiled potatoes with butter and milk appear in English cookbooks by the mid-1700s, and the dish spread with the potato across Europe and North America. Mashing cauliflower in its place is a modern idea, taken up by low-carb home cooks in the United States from the late 1990s onward.

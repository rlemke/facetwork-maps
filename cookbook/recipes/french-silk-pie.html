French silk chocolate pie

A light, airy chocolate filling, beaten until it is smooth as silk, in a baked crust under softly whipped cream.

Dairy-free option 3.7 g net carbs Serves 10 · 1 slice prep 35 min · cook 12 min · rest 4 h

Ingredients

Crust

  • 1½ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (144 g)
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter (57 g), melted
  • 2 tbsp granulated erythritol (24 g)
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt

Filling

  • 85 g unsweetened baking chocolate (100% cacao), melted and cooled
  • ¾ cup unsalted butter (170 g), softened
  • ¾ cup powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (90 g)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • ⅛ tsp fine salt
  • 3 large eggs, pasteurized, at room temperature

Topping

  • 1 cup heavy whipping cream (238 g)
  • 2 tbsp powdered erythritol (confectioners' style) (16 g)
  • 15 g dark chocolate, 85% cacao, shaved

⚠️ Check the label: some dark chocolate contains milk.

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F). Press the crust into a 23 cm (9 in) pie plate and bake 10–12 minutes, until golden. Cool completely.
  2. Beat the softened butter and powdered erythritol with an electric mixer for 3 minutes, until pale and fluffy. Beat in the cooled melted chocolate, vanilla and salt.
  3. Add the eggs one at a time, beating 4–5 minutes on medium-high after each, until the filling is light and no longer grainy. This long beating is what gives the silky texture.
  4. Spread the filling in the cold crust and refrigerate at least 4 hours, until firm.
  5. Just before serving, whip the cream with the powdered erythritol to soft peaks, spread it over the pie and scatter the shaved chocolate on top.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter — use a firm stick-style one.
  • Use coconut cream (canned, unsweetened) instead of heavy whipping cream — chill the can overnight and whip only the solid part.

With dairy-free butter the filling is slightly softer and tastes more of chocolate than of butter; keep it cold until serving.

Tips

  • The eggs are not cooked, so use pasteurized eggs.
  • If the filling looks curdled, the chocolate was too warm or the butter too cold; keep beating and it usually comes together.

Where it comes from

Chocolate cream pie · Maryland, United States · North America · 1951

Despite the name, this is an American pie rather than a French one. It is usually credited to a Maryland home baker's entry in the 1951 Pillsbury Bake-Off, which made it widely known. The original relied on raw eggs beaten into butter and chocolate, which is why modern versions call for pasteurized eggs.

Soft almond turrón (Jijona style)

The soft, crumbly kind of Spanish turrón: roasted almonds ground to a paste with sweetener, lemon and cinnamon, pressed into a bar.

Dairy-free 1.5 g net carbs Serves 16 · 1 slice prep 25 min · cook 15 min · rest 12 h

Ingredients

  • 250 g almonds, blanched
  • ½ cup allulose (96 g)
  • 2 tbsp water (30 g)
  • 1 large egg white
  • 1 tsp lemon zest
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • 0.0625 tsp fine salt

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 165 °C (325 °F). Roast the almonds on a tray for 10–12 minutes, until golden through. Cool.
  2. Grind the almonds in a food processor until they pass the crumb stage and become a thick, oily paste, 3–5 minutes, scraping down often.
  3. Warm the allulose and water in a small pan until it bubbles and reaches 115 °C (240 °F). Do not let it darken.
  4. With the processor running, add the egg white, then pour in the hot syrup, then the zest, cinnamon and salt. Process until evenly combined.
  5. Line a 20 x 10 cm (8 x 4 in) loaf tin with parchment, press the paste in firmly and level it. Cover and leave at room temperature for 12 hours to firm, then slice.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Toast the almonds well: their roasted flavour is the whole character of turrón de Jijona.

Where it comes from

Turrón · Jijona (Xixona) · Southern Europe · 1500s

Turrón, an almond and honey confection, has been made around Jijona and Alicante in eastern Spain for centuries and is documented there from the sixteenth century; its roots are often traced to the sweets of Moorish Spain. Jijona gives its name to the soft, ground-almond kind, while the hard kind with whole almonds is associated with Alicante. This version replaces the honey with allulose.

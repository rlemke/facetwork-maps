Chocolate chip cookies

Chewy-edged almond-flour cookies studded with sugar-free dark chocolate, with a short chill so they hold their shape.

Dairy-free option 1.6 g net carbs Serves 18 · 1 cookie prep 15 min · cook 11 min · rest 30 min

Ingredients

  • ½ cup unsalted butter (114 g), softened
  • ½ cup granulated erythritol (96 g)
  • 2 tbsp allulose (24 g), helps the cookies spread and stay chewy
  • 1 large egg, at room temperature
  • 2 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 2¼ cups almond flour (blanched, finely ground) (216 g)
  • ¼ tsp xanthan gum
  • ½ tsp baking soda
  • ½ tsp fine salt
  • ½ cup sugar-free dark chocolate chips (stevia or erythritol sweetened) (85 g)

⚠️ Many brands contain milk fat; choose one labelled dairy-free.

Method

  1. Beat the butter, erythritol and allulose until pale and fluffy, about 2 minutes. Beat in the egg and vanilla.
  2. Whisk the almond flour, xanthan gum, baking soda and salt together, then stir them into the butter mixture until no dry streaks remain. Fold in the chocolate chips.
  3. Cover and chill the dough for 30 minutes; almond-flour dough is soft and spreads too thin if baked warm.
  4. Heat the oven to 175 °C (350 °F) and line two baking sheets with parchment. Scoop 18 balls of dough, space them 5 cm (2 in) apart and flatten each slightly.
  5. Bake 10–12 minutes, until the edges are golden but the centres still look soft.
  6. Leave on the sheet for 10 minutes before moving; they are fragile until cool and firm up as they set.

Dairy-free version

  • Use dairy-free butter (stick, plant-based) instead of unsalted butter — use a firm stick-style one.

Dairy-free butter gives a slightly softer, flatter cookie with less of a browned-butter flavour; chill the dough the full 30 minutes.

Tips

  • A pinch more salt on top just out of the oven sharpens the chocolate.
  • Allulose browns quickly; check the first tray at 9 minutes.

Where it comes from

Drop cookie · Whitman, Massachusetts · North America · 1930s

The chocolate chip cookie is credited to Ruth Wakefield, who ran the Toll House Inn in Whitman, Massachusetts, and added chopped semi-sweet chocolate to a butter cookie in the late 1930s. Her recipe spread through newspapers and cookbooks, and chocolate makers soon began selling chips made for baking.

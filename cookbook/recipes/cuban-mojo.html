Cuban mojo

Mojo criollo: a garlic-heavy citrus marinade and sauce with cumin and oregano, for roast pork.

Dairy-free 2.4 g net carbs Serves 8 · marinade for 225 g of pork prep 10 min · rest 12 h

Ingredients

  • 8 garlic cloves, mashed to a paste with the salt
  • 2 tsp fine salt (12 g)
  • ¼ cup fresh orange juice (60 g)
  • 2 tbsp fresh lime juice (30 g)
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice (15 g)
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper
  • ⅓ cup olive oil (71 g)

Nothing here is a low-carb stand-in, so a conventional version would be much the same.

Contains: none of the common allergen groups.

Method

  1. Mash the garlic with the salt into a paste, using a mortar or the flat of a knife.
  2. Stir in the citrus juices, cumin, oregano and pepper.
  3. Warm the olive oil until it shimmers, take it off the heat and pour it over the garlic mixture (it will sizzle), then let cool.
  4. Use about two-thirds to marinate 1.8 kg (4 lb) of pork shoulder overnight. Roast at 160 °C (325 °F) to 93–96 °C (200–205 °F) for pulled pork, or to 63 °C (145 °F) for sliced pork tenderloin. Spoon the rest over the cooked meat.

Dairy-free as written

Tips

  • Mojo is made with sour (Seville) orange. Here a small amount of sweet orange juice plus lime and lemon stands in for it; if you can find sour oranges, use 1/3 cup of their juice instead of all three.
  • The carbs shown count the whole batch; only what clings to the meat is eaten.

Where it comes from

Mojo · Havana · Latin America & Caribbean · 1800s

Mojo criollo is the garlic and sour-orange sauce behind Cuban roast pork. Its name is shared with the mojo sauces of the Canary Islands, and Canarian immigration to Cuba is the usual explanation for the link, though the Cuban version, built on sour orange, is its own sauce. It travelled to Florida with Cuban emigrants in the twentieth century.
